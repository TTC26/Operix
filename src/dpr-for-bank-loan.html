DPR for bank loan: format, contents and checklist

A Detailed Project Report (DPR) is the document a bank uses to decide whether your project can repay the loan. A good DPR answers three questions clearly: what you will build, what it costs and who pays for it, and how the cash flows repay the debt.

What banks look for

  • Promoter strength: experience in the line of business, own contribution and net worth.
  • Realistic project cost: machinery and building costs backed by quotations and estimates.
  • Balanced funding: project cost exactly equal to means of finance, with an adequate promoter margin.
  • Repayment capacity: Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) comfortably above the lender's minimum in every year of the loan.
  • Believable assumptions: capacity utilisation, prices and costs that match the industry and your track record.

Standard DPR format

  1. Executive summary: project, cost, funding, key ratios
  2. Promoter profile and company details
  3. Products, installed capacity and capacity utilisation
  4. Market and industry analysis
  5. Manufacturing process and technical feasibility
  6. Location, land, building and utilities
  7. Cost of project and means of finance
  8. Operating assumptions: raw material, manpower, power, expenses
  9. Projected profit and loss, balance sheet and cash flow (5 to 10 years)
  10. Ratio analysis and DSCR
  11. Loan repayment schedule
  12. Working capital assessment
  13. Sensitivity and break-even analysis
  14. Risk analysis and mitigation
  15. Statutory approvals and implementation schedule
  16. Annexures: quotations, KYC, licences, past financials

The key numbers

DSCR(PAT + depreciation + interest) ÷ (principal + interest)Many lenders look for an average around 1.5× and a minimum around 1.25×; check your bank's norm.
Debt / equityTerm debt ÷ promoter fundsLower is safer; limits vary by lender and scheme.

Common mistakes

  • Project cost and means of finance that don't match.
  • A balance sheet that doesn't balance in later years.
  • Sales that exceed installed capacity.
  • Year-1 revenue far above the last actual turnover with no explanation.
  • Machinery without quotations, or quotations without freight, installation and taxes.
  • No moratorium in the repayment schedule when the project takes months to start.

Frequently asked questions

How many years of projections should a DPR have?

Usually the full loan tenure, often 5 to 7 years for a term loan. Bizvora lets you choose 5 to 10 years.

Is a DPR the same as a project report?

In practice the terms are used interchangeably. A DPR is the detailed version, with full financial projections and appraisal ratios.

Who can prepare a DPR?

Anyone can prepare it, but the numbers must be consistent and backed by quotations. Many banks also ask for a CA's certification of projections.
